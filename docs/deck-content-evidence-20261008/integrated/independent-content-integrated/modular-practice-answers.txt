1. A plant has light, water and carbon dioxide, but makes much less chlorophyll. Which direct change is most likely in the light reactions?
Concept: Light capture
Your first answer: Less carbon dioxide is absorbed directly by chlorophyll
First try: needs review
Correct answer: Less light energy is absorbed to drive electron transfer
Explanation: Chlorophyll absorbs light energy that drives electron transfer in photosynthesis. Making much less chlorophyll reduces this light capture; chlorophyll does not absorb carbon dioxide or split oxygen.
Apply the idea: This links a visible input (light) to the first energy-conversion step.
Practice answer: More glucose is split to replace light absorption in photosystems
Practice result: keep reviewing

2. A human muscle cell has glucose available, but oxygen becomes limiting. What is the strongest prediction for its mitochondrial electron transport chain?
Concept: Respiration
Your first answer: Electron flow slows because oxygen is the chain's final electron acceptor
First try: correct
Correct answer: Electron flow slows because oxygen is the chain's final electron acceptor
Explanation: In aerobic respiration, oxygen accepts electrons at the end of the mitochondrial electron transport chain. Limited oxygen constrains that pathway; cells may use other processes, but those are not the same as aerobic respiration.
Apply the idea: The stored energy in glucose is released through linked pathways; oxygen's role is at the chain's end.

3. Which statement best describes the role of the Calvin cycle?
Concept: Light capture
Your first answer: It uses chlorophyll to split water and store light energy as NADPH
First try: needs review
Correct answer: It uses ATP and NADPH from light reactions to build carbohydrate from carbon dioxide
Explanation: The Calvin cycle uses energy carriers produced by the light reactions to fix carbon dioxide into carbohydrate building blocks; it does not capture light directly.
Apply the idea: Keep the two linked stages distinct: light capture supplies chemical energy; carbon fixation uses it.
Practice answer: It uses ATP and NADPH from light reactions to build carbohydrate from carbon dioxide
Practice result: correct on retry

4. Why is ATP useful as an immediate energy carrier even though cells store much more energy in fuel molecules?
Concept: ATP transfer
Your first answer: Its hydrolysis can power cellular work through coupled chemical reactions
First try: correct
Correct answer: Its hydrolysis can power cellular work through coupled chemical reactions
Explanation: ATP hydrolysis can be coupled to endergonic cellular processes. Cells continually regenerate ATP; it is an immediate transferable energy currency, not a long-term bulk store.
Apply the idea: Distinguish the fuel reservoir (for example, glucose) from the frequently regenerated spendable carrier (ATP).

5. Which comparison best connects photosynthesis and cellular respiration?
Concept: ATP transfer
Your first answer: They run the same chemical reactions in opposite directions inside the same cell compartment
First try: needs review
Correct answer: Photosynthesis stores captured light energy in sugars; respiration transfers some of that energy to ATP
Explanation: Photosynthesis stores captured light energy in organic molecules; cellular respiration transfers energy from organic molecules into ATP. The pathways are connected but are not simply identical reverse reactions.
Apply the idea: Use the model to explain why food webs depend on photosynthetic energy capture and why cells need respiration.
Practice answer: Photosynthesis stores captured light energy in sugars; respiration transfers some of that energy to ATP
Practice result: correct on retry

6. A learner says, “Plants get their usable energy from soil.” Which correction best connects photosynthesis to stored chemical energy?
Concept: Stored sugar
Your first answer: Light supplies energy to build sugars; soil minerals supply nutrients for growth
First try: correct
Correct answer: Light supplies energy to build sugars; soil minerals supply nutrients for growth
Explanation: Photosynthesis uses light energy to build energy-rich carbohydrates from carbon dioxide (with water involved in the overall process). Soil minerals support growth but are not the energy source for sugar synthesis.
Apply the idea: Explain the energy path: light → chemical energy in sugars; nutrients and energy are different things.
