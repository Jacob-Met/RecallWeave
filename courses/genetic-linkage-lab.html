A continuation of Mendelian inheritance

Same genotype.
Different gametes.

AaBb does not tell the whole story. Place the alleles on their homologs, set a recombinant-gamete fraction, and follow exact probabilities through a testcross.

A deliberately small model: hypothetical plants, two autosomal loci, equal allele segregation and an AaBb × aabb testcross. The percentage is given, not estimated from data. This is not a chromosome map or a prediction for a particular organism.

01 / Set the arrangement

Choose a phase

AB / ab is coupling; Ab / aB is repulsion. Each slash separates the two homologs.

Whole numbers from 0 to 50. Each of the two recombinant classes gets half this percentage.

Interpret with care

A fraction is not an event count.

The recombinant percentage describes gametes. It is not a count of crossover events or a physical distance.

Likewise, a probability of 1/10 is not a promise of exactly one occurrence in every ten offspring. Finite observations can vary.

The earlier Mendelian lab deliberately assumes independent assortment. This companion adds a different, explicit set of gamete weights without changing that lesson.
