## Intended learner and scope

Twelve original four-option questions, three per named concept: membrane
selectivity, passive transport, osmosis and active transport. They target an
introductory high-school or first-college biology learner. No calculus, clinical
case or membrane-potential calculation is needed. Because adaptive question
selection does not enforce prerequisite order, every stem must supply the
conditions needed to answer it independently.

## Scientific acceptance

Each item needs one uniquely defensible correct option under its explicit
conditions, a worked explanation that follows from those conditions, and three
false alternatives. The reviewer will check all options, not only the key.
Quantities must use consistent units, fictional values and an explicit initial
versus later/equilibrium time frame when that changes the result.

Particular ambiguity controls are:

- Distinguish a pure lipid bilayer from a membrane containing transport proteins.
  State whether the relevant substance is charged or neutral and whether a
  suitable pathway is present. Avoid treating all small substances alike.
- Separate net diffusion from continuing microscopic motion. A concentration
  statement alone must not determine a charged solute's direction when an
  unspecified electrical force could oppose it.
- A transport protein does not itself establish active transport. Classify the
  process from the energy coupling and direction under the given gradients.
- For water movement, identify the nonpenetrating dissolved particles, relative
  concentrations, membrane permeability and initial pressure conditions. State
  dissociation assumptions if a count depends on them. A restrained plant cell
  can develop opposing pressure; do not imply that the wall prevents all water
  exchange or that final solute concentrations must always become equal.
- Distinguish direct ATP-driven pumping from energy stored in an ion gradient
  and from the immediate coupling used by a cotransporter. A changed upstream
  ATP supply does not, without timing/gradient conditions, prove an immediate
  disappearance of an already established driving gradient.

These are review criteria, not a requirement to expand the proposed course to
advanced thermodynamics or additional topics.

## Learning value and answer cues

A distractor should express a specific misconception that can be repaired by
the explanation or the companion guide. An irrelevant or obviously absurd
option is weak evidence of discrimination. The guide's misconception rationale
must agree with the actual displayed option. Explanations should describe the
causal distinction and avoid claiming a measurement of learner ability.

A transfer prompt must change a meaningful condition, representation or
context and invite application of the same principle. It must be answerable
from the taught model. Merely asking for the original correct phrase is
insufficient. Open transfer responses are not automatically scientifically
graded by the application.

The author's balanced key contract is three answers in each of four positions.
The reviewer will also measure unique longest/shortest option heuristics and
inspect grammatical, detail, hedging and repeated-word clues. These descriptive
counts are cue diagnostics, not learning-effectiveness statistics. Parallel
alternatives and distinct misconception choices matter beyond length alone.

   every item before consulting the author's key or the guide.

   explanations, distractor rationales, transfer prompts and source attribution.
   Preserve any concrete disagreement and send it promptly to the owner.

   and parent's separate current-importer/browser receiving must qualify the
   actual modular and standalone learner flow when #7 is ready. A pure format
   pass cannot stand in for that integration.
