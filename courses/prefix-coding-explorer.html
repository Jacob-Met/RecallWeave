1Choose symbol counts

Use 2–8 distinct labels, each with 1–24 Unicode characters and some non-whitespace text. Spaces and letter case remain part of a label. Counts are whole numbers from 1 to 10,000.

What stays fixed

Left edges are 0; right edges are 1. Equal counts use a reproducible order: original labels in Unicode character order, then combined trees in their creation order. Other tie choices can give different, equally good codes.

Editing the counts or labels starts a new tree and clears the bit stream. Your inputs stay here until you change them or close the page.

3Read the bits back

Use up to 4,096 bits without spaces. An empty stream means no symbols. A stream that ends inside a codeword is incomplete.

    Why this works—and what the comparison means

    Huffman’s greedy construction minimizes the weighted payload length among binary prefix codes for these symbol counts. The decoder also needs the codebook and framing. Their costs are excluded here, so fewer payload bits do not promise a smaller real file. Prefix coding preserves the exact symbols; it does not recover a damaged or incomplete message.
