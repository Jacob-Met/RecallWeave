1. In a fictional puzzle log, option A records 42 successes in 70 newcomer attempts and 18 successes in 20 experienced-player attempts. Which fraction gives A's success rate among newcomers?
Concept: Denominators
Your first answer: 70 / 90
First try: needs review
Correct answer: 42 / 70
Explanation: The requested group is newcomers using A, so its successes and attempts are the numerator and denominator: 42/70 = 60%. The other group's 20 attempts do not belong in this conditional rate.
Apply the idea: For a percentage you encounter, name exactly which records belong in its denominator before comparing it with another percentage.
Practice answer: not recorded.

2. In another fictional sample, newcomer rates are A: 8/10 and B: 6/10. Experienced-player rates are A: 4/10 and B: 9/10. Does this show the strict pattern of both within-group comparisons reversing when pooled?
Concept: Group composition
Your first answer: No, because the two within-group comparisons point opposite ways.
First try: correct
Correct answer: No, because the two within-group comparisons point opposite ways.
Explanation: A is higher among newcomers, while B is higher among experienced players. There is no single strict direction shared by both groups to reverse. B's pooled 15/20 exceeds A's 12/20, but that does not change the conflicting group directions.
Apply the idea: Construct a case where one subgroup is tied. Explain why a tie should not be silently labeled as a strict advantage.

3. A's pooled rate is exactly 2/3. B's pooled rate is 77/100. By approximately how many percentage points is B higher?
Concept: Denominators
Your first answer: 10.33 percentage points
First try: correct
Correct answer: 10.33 percentage points
Explanation: The difference in proportions is 77/100 - 2/3 = 31/300. Multiply by 100 to obtain 10 1/3 percentage points. A relative percentage increase would use a further division by A's rate and is a different quantity.
Apply the idea: A rate changes from 40% to 50%. State the change in percentage points and, separately, the relative percentage increase.

4. Option A has 42 successes in 70 newcomer attempts and 18 in 20 experienced-player attempts. What is its success rate over all 90 recorded attempts?
Concept: Denominators
Your first answer: 75 / 100, exactly 75%
First try: needs review
Correct answer: 60 / 90, about 66.67%
Explanation: Add the successes and add the attempts: (42+18)/(70+20) = 60/90 = 2/3. Averaging 60% and 90% equally would answer a different question because the groups contain 70 and 20 attempts.
Apply the idea: Two batches contain 1 success in 2 attempts and 8 in 8. Calculate the pooled rate from counts, then explain why the unweighted average of the two rates differs.
Practice answer: not recorded.

5. The same fictional logs give A versus B rates of 60% versus 50% among newcomers, 90% versus 80% among experienced players, and about 66.67% versus 77% when pooled. Which statement is supported by those recorded counts?
Concept: Group composition
Your first answer: A has a higher rate in both groups; B has a higher pooled rate.
First try: correct
Correct answer: A has a higher rate in both groups; B has a higher pooled rate.
Explanation: The association reverses direction between both within-group comparisons and the pooled comparison. This is the strict two-group form of Simpson's reversal used in the explorer. The counts describe recorded outcomes; they do not identify what changing an option would cause for a player.
Apply the idea: Describe a reversal using only observed fractions. Then write a separate causal question that those fractions alone do not answer.

6. Keep the first sample's group rates fixed: newcomers A 60%, B 50%; experienced players A 90%, B 80%. What are the reference rates if both options use a 50% newcomer / 50% experienced mix?
Concept: Reference mix
Your first answer: A 75%; B 65%
First try: correct
Correct answer: A 75%; B 65%
Explanation: Apply the same weights to both options: A = 0.5×0.60 + 0.5×0.90 = 0.75, and B = 0.5×0.50 + 0.5×0.80 = 0.65. These reference rates answer a chosen same-mix question. They do not replace the recorded pooled rates or change the original counts.
Apply the idea: Why is selecting a reference population part of defining the comparison, rather than discovering the uniquely correct population from the table?

7. A has no recorded newcomer attempts (0 successes out of 0) and 9 successes in 10 experienced-player attempts. What can the explorer report for A at a reference mix of 50% newcomers and 50% experienced players?
Concept: Reference mix
Your first answer: 45%: treat unobserved newcomers as having zero successes.
First try: needs review
Correct answer: Unavailable: one positive-weight group has no observed rate.
Explanation: Zero attempts do not establish a 0% success rate. The newcomer component is needed at this reference mix but unavailable. Assigning it zero or borrowing the other group's rate would add an assumption absent from the data.
Apply the idea: Describe the difference between zero successes in ten attempts and zero successes in zero attempts. Which one establishes an observed rate?
Practice answer: not recorded.

8. A's newcomer rate is 60% and experienced-player rate is 90%. Apply a reference mix of 20% newcomers and 80% experienced players. What reference rate results?
Concept: Reference mix
Your first answer: 84%
First try: correct
Correct answer: 84%
Explanation: Use 0.20×0.60 + 0.80×0.90 = 0.84. B's corresponding 50% and 80% group rates would give 74%, retaining A's 10-percentage-point advantage. A weighted average using the same nonnegative weights cannot reverse a strict advantage present in every included group.
Apply the idea: Choose another common mix and compute both reference rates. Keep the original sample counts and pooled rates written alongside the new comparison.

9. A still has 0/0 newcomer attempts and 9/10 experienced-player attempts. Now the reference mix contains 0% newcomers and 100% experienced players. What reference rate can be computed without filling in a missing rate?
Concept: Reference mix
Your first answer: 90%
First try: correct
Correct answer: 90%
Explanation: Only the experienced-player rate has positive weight, so the reference rate is 9/10 = 90%. The newcomer rate remains unavailable; it has not been inferred. Any mix assigning newcomers positive weight would require information not present in these counts.
Apply the idea: Move the reference weight from 100% experienced to 99%. Explain why the missing group's small positive weight still prevents this calculation.

10. The puzzle counts come from observational logs, with no stated assignment procedure. To investigate whether switching a player from B to A would improve success, which next step addresses the missing causal information?
Concept: Causal questions
Your first answer: Force a fifty-fifty group mix and treat its difference as the effect.
First try: needs review
Correct answer: Specify the intervention and examine assignment, timing and common causes.
Explanation: A causal question needs a defined intervention and justified design or assumptions. Assignment, relevant common causes, and the timing of variables matter. Neither a larger pooled rate nor a standardized descriptive rate alone supplies those assumptions.
Apply the idea: For a comparison of two study methods, name one design feature and one pre-method variable you would examine before interpreting a rate difference causally.
Practice answer: not recorded.

11. Suppose the chosen method can change whether a player completes optional practice, and practice can affect final puzzle success. The question concerns the method's total effect, including that practice pathway. Should comparing only within post-method practice groups automatically answer it?
Concept: Causal questions
Your first answer: No; such grouping can remove part of the pathway being studied.
First try: correct
Correct answer: No; such grouping can remove part of the pathway being studied.
Explanation: A group variable can lie on the pathway from the method to the outcome. Conditioning on it can exclude part of the total effect being asked about. This does not make the raw pooled association automatically causal either; the study design and other causal assumptions still matter.
Apply the idea: Before using a variable to form groups, ask when it was measured, whether the method could change it, and which effect or descriptive population you actually want to compare.

12. A has newcomer rate 60% (42/70) and experienced-player rate 90% (18/20). B has newcomer rate 50% (5/10) and experienced-player rate 80% (72/90). Which group-composition statement explains how B can have the higher pooled rate?
Concept: Group composition
Your first answer: B gives greater weight to experienced players, its higher-rate group.
First try: correct
Correct answer: B gives greater weight to experienced players, its higher-rate group.
Explanation: Experienced players account for 20/90 of A's attempts but 90/100 of B's. B therefore places much more of its own sample weight on a group with higher success rates. A's pooled rate is about 66.67%; B's is 77%. All four conditional rates and both pooled rates can be correct.
Apply the idea: Keep every within-group rate fixed. Which option's pooled rate would increase if a larger share of its attempts came from its higher-rate group? Explain the weighting, without claiming new observations.
