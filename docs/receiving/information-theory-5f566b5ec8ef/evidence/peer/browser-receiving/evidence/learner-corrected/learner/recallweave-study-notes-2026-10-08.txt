1. The joint table is [3,0]; [0,1]. Either label determines the other perfectly, and H(X) = H(Y) is approximately 0.811278 bits. What is I(X;Y)?
Concept: Mutual information
Your first answer: Approximately 0.811278 bits: the information removes the other's entire original uncertainty.
First try: correct
Correct answer: Approximately 0.811278 bits: the information removes the other's entire original uncertainty.
Explanation: I(X;Y) = H(Y) - H(Y|X). The conditional entropy is zero because X determines Y, so the shared information equals H(Y), approximately 0.811278 bits. Perfect prediction need not provide one bit: the amount depends on the uncertainty present before the observation. The same identity holds with X and Y exchanged.

First try: correct
Correct answer: A particular observation can increase uncertainty; the average H(Y|X) is still at most H(Y).
Explanation: The inequality concerns the average over observations, not every conditional distribution separately. 8. X has four named categories, but all 7 cards carry X1; the other category counts are zero. What is H(X)?

First try: correct
Correct answer: 0 bits, because the X label is already certain.
Explanation: The only positive-probability X label has probability 1 and surprisal 0. Its contribution is zero, and every zero-probability entropy summand is defined as zero. Thus H(X) = 0. 9. X takes two labels with probabilities 3/4 and 1/4. Which expression is its entropy in bits?
Concept: Surprise and entropy
Your first answer: (3/4)log2(4/3) + (1/4)log2(4), approximately 0.811278.
First try: correct
Correct answer: (3/4)log2(4/3) + (1/4)log2(4), approximately 0.811278.
Explanation: Entropy is the probability-weighted average of surprisal. The common label contributes (3/4)log2(4/3), and the rare label contributes (1/4) times 2. Their sum is about 0.811278 bits. A fair two-label variable has entropy 1 bit; uneven probabilities reduce this average uncertainty.
