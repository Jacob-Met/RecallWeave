1. Use the composite midpoint rule for f(x) = x² on [0, 2] with n = 2 equal elementary subintervals. Which calculation is correct?
Concept: Nodes and weights
Your first answer: 1 × [f(1/2) + f(3/2)] = 5/2
First try: correct
Correct answer: 1 × [f(1/2) + f(3/2)] = 5/2
Explanation: Each subinterval has width h = (2 - 0)/2 = 1. Their midpoints are 1/2 and 3/2. The sampled heights are 1/4 and 9/4, so the estimate is 1 × (1/4 + 9/4) = 5/2. A midpoint rule uses the middle of each subinterval, not just the midpoint of the entire interval.

3. A composite trapezoid estimate on [0, 4] uses n = 4 equal subintervals. What are the aggregated weights on f(0), f(1), f(2), f(3), f(4)?
Concept: Nodes and weights
Your first answer: 1/2, 1, 1, 1, 1/2
First try: correct
Correct answer: 1/2, 1, 1, 1, 1/2
Explanation: Here h = 1. Each small trapezoid contributes h/2 times each of its endpoint values. An interior node belongs to two adjacent trapezoids and therefore receives total weight 1; each outer endpoint receives 1/2. These weights sum to 4, which makes every constant function integrate exactly over the width-4 interval.

5. For f(x) = 5x⁴ - 9x² on [-1, 1], the exact integral is -4. The trapezoid estimates are -4 at n = 2 and -63/16 at n = 4. Which conclusion follows?
Concept: Error and refinement
Your first answer: This refinement loses a coincidentally exact answer; its new signed error is 1/16
First try: correct
Correct answer: This refinement loses a coincidentally exact answer; its new signed error is 1/16
Explanation: At n = 2, separate polynomial error contributions happen to cancel. At n = 4, the estimate minus the integral is -63/16 - (-64/16) = 1/16. More subintervals do not guarantee a smaller error at every individual refinement. This counterexample is compatible with eventual convergence as the mesh becomes fine.

8. Which of these guarantees applies to every polynomial of degree at most three, on any admitted interval and mesh, when the arithmetic is exact?

Your first answer: All three rules are exact only if the integral is positive
First try: needs review
Correct answer: Composite Simpson quadrature is exact
Explanation: Composite Simpson quadrature integrates every polynomial of degree at most three exactly. The midpoint and trapezoid rules guarantee exactness for degree at most one.
