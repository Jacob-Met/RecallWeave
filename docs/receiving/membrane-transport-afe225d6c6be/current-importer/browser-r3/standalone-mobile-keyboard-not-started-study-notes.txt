REVIEW THE CONNECTIONS

1. A phospholipid bilayer separates two watery solutions. Which arrangement is most stable for its phospholipids?
Concept: Membrane selectivity
Your first answer: Hydrophilic heads face the water; hydrophobic tails face one another.
First try: correct
Correct answer: Hydrophilic heads face the water; hydrophobic tails face one another.
Explanation: Each phospholipid has a water-interacting head and nonpolar tails. In a bilayer, heads face the watery solution on each surface while tails pack into the interior. Both surfaces therefore expose heads. The bilayer is not a single sheet with heads on one side and tails on the other.
Apply the idea: Use the head-and-tail arrangement to explain why a cut edge of a bilayer is energetically unfavorable in water.

2. A membrane is permeable to neutral solute X in dilute, ideal solutions. Initially, X is 8 mmol/L on side A and 2 mmol/L on side B. Temperature and pressure match, and no pump acts on X. Which describes its initial diffusion?
Concept: Passive transport
Your first answer: More X crosses from A to B than from B to A.
First try: correct
Correct answer: More X crosses from A to B than from B to A.
Explanation: Individual X molecules move randomly and can cross in either direction. Initially, the larger concentration on side A produces more A-to-B crossings on average than B-to-A crossings. This net movement down the concentration gradient is passive; it does not require a pump or ATP.
Apply the idea: If X later reaches 5 mmol/L on both sides, what changes about net flow and what keeps happening at the molecular scale?

3. An artificial membrane contains phospholipids but no transport proteins. Oxygen and sodium ions are both more concentrated outside. Which comparison is best for direct passage through the bilayer?
Concept: Membrane selectivity
Your first answer: Oxygen crosses readily; sodium ions cross very poorly.
First try: correct
Correct answer: Oxygen crosses readily; sodium ions cross very poorly.
Explanation: Oxygen is small and nonpolar, so it can pass through the bilayer's nonpolar interior. Sodium is a charged ion and crosses that interior very poorly without a suitable protein route. Particle size alone does not predict membrane permeability. Oxygen's passive passage does not require ATP hydrolysis.
Apply the idea: Would adding an open sodium-selective channel mainly change sodium's crossing route or its charge? Explain.

4. Two closed compartments have reached diffusion equilibrium for neutral solute X across a permeable membrane. The solutions are ideal, their temperatures match, and no pressure difference acts on X. What continues at equilibrium?
Concept: Passive transport
Your first answer: Molecules cross both ways at equal average rates.
First try: correct
Correct answer: Molecules cross both ways at equal average rates.
Explanation: Diffusion equilibrium is dynamic. X molecules still move and cross the membrane, but the average opposing crossing rates balance. The net flux is zero even though molecular motion continues. Neither a membrane closure nor an ATP-driven balancing mechanism is needed.
Apply the idea: Would seeing one labeled X molecule cross the membrane prove that net diffusion is occurring? Explain what would need to be compared.

5. An engineered membrane pump moves a neutral solute from 1 mmol/L on side A to 10 mmol/L on side B, with equal pressure and no other coupled solute. The pump hydrolyzes ATP during each transport cycle. How is this transport classified?
Concept: Active transport
Your first answer: Primary active transport, with ATP directly powering uphill movement.
First try: correct
Correct answer: Primary active transport, with ATP directly powering uphill movement.
Explanation: The pump moves the neutral solute from lower to higher concentration and directly couples that movement to ATP hydrolysis. This is primary active transport. Facilitated and simple diffusion cannot sustain the stated uphill net flux without an energy source. Secondary active transport would couple transport to another solute's downhill movement, which the prompt excludes.
Apply the idea: If ATP runs out but the 1-to-10 concentration difference remains, could this pump sustain the same uphill net flux without another energy source? Why?

6. A cell has more potassium ions inside than outside, and its interior is electrically negative relative to the outside. A potassium channel opens. No magnitudes of the concentration difference or voltage are given. What can be concluded about the initial net potassium movement?
Concept: Active transport
Your first answer: It must be inward, because electrical attraction always dominates.
First try: needs review
Correct answer: Its direction needs both the chemical and electrical effects to be compared.
Explanation: The potassium concentration difference favors outward movement, while the negative interior electrically favors inward movement of positive potassium ions. These contributions oppose each other. Their relative sizes determine the net electrochemical driving force; knowing their directions alone does not establish whether the net flux is inward, outward, or zero. The open channel itself is a passive route.
Apply the idea: Predict the initial direction instead if positive sodium ions are more concentrated outside and the interior remains negative, assuming an open sodium channel. Which way do both contributions point?

7. An operating cotransporter brings a neutral nutrient into a cell against its concentration gradient by coupling it to sodium moving inward down its electrochemical gradient. The cotransporter does not hydrolyze ATP. The only pump maintaining the sodium gradient stops, but the cotransporter stays intact and the gradient still exists initially. No other process restores it. What is the best initial prediction?
Concept: Active transport
Your first answer: Uphill uptake can continue using the sodium gradient, then weaken as that gradient dissipates.
First try: correct
Correct answer: Uphill uptake can continue using the sodium gradient, then weaken as that gradient dissipates.
Explanation: The cotransporter's immediate energy source is sodium's electrochemical gradient. Stopping the pump does not erase energy already stored in that gradient, so secondary active uptake can continue while the gradient is sufficient. As the gradient dissipates without maintenance, it provides less driving force for uphill nutrient uptake. The pump and cotransporter have related but different energy roles.
Apply the idea: Why can inhibiting an ATP pump eventually reduce the activity of a different transporter that never binds ATP?

8. In an idealized model, a flexible artificial cell has 200 mmol/L of the same nonpenetrating, non-dissociating solute inside and 300 mmol/L outside. Only water can cross; hydrostatic pressure is initially equal on both sides. What happens first?
Concept: Osmosis
Your first answer: Net water leaves the cell, so its volume initially decreases.
First try: correct
Correct answer: Net water leaves the cell, so its volume initially decreases.
Explanation: The outside has the greater concentration of retained solute particles. With equal initial pressure and a water-permeable membrane, net water movement is initially outward toward that solution. The cell initially loses volume. Osmosis does not require the solute itself to cross; this prompt explicitly prevents it from doing so.
Apply the idea: Repeat the initial prediction with the inside and outside concentrations exchanged. Which variable sets the direction when pressure remains equal?

9. A water-permeable membrane separates equal-volume ideal solutions. Side A has 100 mmol/L of nonpenetrating solute X plus 100 mmol/L of nonpenetrating solute Y. Side B has 200 mmol/L of nonpenetrating solute Z. None dissociates; pressure and temperature match. What is the initial net water flow?
Concept: Osmosis
Your first answer: No net water flow, although water crosses in both directions.
First try: correct
Correct answer: No net water flow, although water crosses in both directions.
Explanation: Count all retained particles per volume: side A has 100 + 100 = 200 mmol/L and side B has 200 mmol/L. Under these ideal, equal-pressure conditions there is no initial net water flow. Counting the number of solute names, or comparing Z with only one component of A, gives the wrong comparison. Water still crosses both ways.
Apply the idea: If Z instead dissociated into two retained particles per formula unit while the stated formula-unit concentrations stayed the same, how would you revise the particle count and initial water direction?

10. Aquaporins, which are water channels, are added to a membrane while its solute concentrations and pressures are held fixed. There is already a nonzero osmotic driving force. The solute cannot cross. What is expected?
Concept: Membrane selectivity
Your first answer: Water flows in reverse; solute movement stays unchanged.
First try: needs review
Correct answer: Water flows faster in the same direction; solute stays blocked.
Explanation: Aquaporins increase the membrane's permeability to water. With the driving force held fixed, this permits faster net water movement in its existing direction. A water-selective route does not make the specified solute permeable, reverse the driving force, or act as an ATP-powered pump.
Apply the idea: Explain why removing aquaporins can slow osmosis without making a lipid membrane absolutely impermeable to water.
Practice answer: not recorded.

11. A neutral nutrient is more concentrated outside a cell than inside. Its entry requires a specific carrier protein, but the carrier does not use ATP or couple entry to another solute. What best describes the uptake?
Concept: Passive transport
Your first answer: Facilitated diffusion down the nutrient's concentration gradient.
First try: correct
Correct answer: Facilitated diffusion down the nutrient's concentration gradient.
Explanation: A carrier can help a solute move down its own concentration gradient without supplying energy. That is facilitated diffusion. A protein route distinguishes it from simple diffusion through the lipid bilayer. Direct ATP use or coupling to another solute's gradient would describe different mechanisms, neither of which is present here.
Apply the idea: If this carrier is the only route and external nutrient concentration rises while carrier number stays fixed, why might uptake eventually approach a maximum rate?

12. A plant cell with an intact, rigid wall is placed in a solution with less nonpenetrating solute than its cytoplasm. Water can cross the cell membrane, but the solute cannot, and the wall limits expansion. As water enters, what most directly opposes further net entry?
Concept: Osmosis
Your first answer: Loss of random water motion as the cytoplasm becomes crowded.
First try: needs review
Correct answer: Rising hydrostatic pressure as the cell presses against its wall.
Explanation: The wall resists expansion, so water entry raises the cell's hydrostatic pressure, called turgor pressure in this setting. That pressure opposes further net entry and can balance the osmotic effect of the retained solute. Solute concentrations need not become equal for net water movement to stop. Water molecules continue moving.
Apply the idea: Could net water entry stop while the solute concentration inside remains higher than outside? Explain what could balance that concentration effect.
