The two modal clocks

Angular frequency

+ / in-phase basis vector (1, 1)

—rad/s · ω₊ = √(k/m)

− / opposite basis vector (1, −1)

—rad/s · ω₋ = √((k + 2c)/m)

At c = 0 the frequencies are equal. We keep the sum/difference basis here; the normal-mode basis is not unique in this degenerate case.

Each mode evolves independently. A mass generally combines both frequencies; a generic initial condition need not transfer all its energy to the other mass.

E₊ and E₋ are individually constant. The physical kinetic and spring energies can vary. The two decompositions describe the same total; do not add them together.

Readings use eight significant digits. The observation JSON retains the model’s full numeric precision and sampled trajectory.

Coordinate convention

A half-sum, a half-difference.

q₊ = (x₁ + x₂)/2
q₋ = (x₁ − x₂)/2
x₁ = q₊ + q₋ · x₂ = q₊ − q₋
u₊ = (v₁ + v₂)/2
u₋ = (v₁ − v₂)/2

Each modal coordinate has effective mass 2m in this convention. Displacements are measured from equilibrium. The ideal springs are unstrained at equilibrium.

Equations of motion

Coupling cancels in the sum.

m ẍ₁ = −k x₁ + c(x₂ − x₁)
m ẍ₂ = −k x₂ + c(x₁ − x₂)
q̈₊ + (k/m)q₊ = 0
q̈₋ + ((k + 2c)/m)q₋ = 0

The + mode leaves the coupling spring unextended. The − mode changes its extension and has the larger frequency when c > 0.

Energy bookkeeping

Two descriptions, one E.

T = ½m(v₁² + v₂²)
V = ½k(x₁² + x₂²) + ½c(x₂ − x₁)²
E₊ = m u₊² + k q₊²
E₋ = m u₋² + (k + 2c)q₋²
E = T + V = E₊ + E₋

The ideal solution has no damping or external drive. Small displayed drift reflects floating-point evaluation, not a dissipative model.
