01 / THE CLOCK

Whole cycles can disappear.

At times t = n / fs, adding one entire sample rate to a cosine’s frequency adds 2πn to its phase. Cosine repeats, so the observations agree for every integer n.

cos(2π(f + fs)n / fs)
= cos(2πfn / fs)
Try 9 Hz at 8 samples/sec.

The 9 Hz reference has the same samples as 1 Hz. Change the reference to 1 Hz: the lab now shows 9 Hz as a different matching alternative. More samples at the same integer times cannot distinguish this pair.

02 / THE ASSUMPTION

The boundary needs care.

With a known band limit B, the ideal strict condition is fs > 2B. It concerns a complete ideal sample sequence. A finite plot does not establish the band limit or prove practical recovery.

At fs = 8 and f = 4:
sin(2π · 4n/8) = sin(πn) = 0
Why use a sine example?

That signal varies between samples yet looks identical to zero at every sample time. The lab uses a zero-phase cosine, which instead alternates +1 and −1 at this boundary. One specified phase does not justify arbitrary-phase recovery.

03 / THE ACQUISITION

Prevent the fold upstream.

Unwanted high-frequency content can fold into the band you want. An appropriate anti-alias filter attenuates it before sampling. A later filter cannot distinguish causes that already gave identical readings.

Input → anti-alias filter
→ sampler → digital processing
Can a faster rate help?

For a new acquisition of the authored 9 Hz reference, 24 samples/sec puts 9 Hz below the 12 Hz half-rate. It does not repair an older recording. This lab does not design a physical filter or test real equipment.
