What this model assumes

  • Repeat readings are independent observations of one stable quantity. Their scatter estimates repeatability.
  • A recognized additive calibration correction has been applied. Its remaining uncertainty is shared across every reading.
  • The correction's stated ±a range is modeled as rectangular, and its uncertainty is independent of the repeatability component.
  • Other effects are outside this two-component teaching model. A real uncertainty budget may need resolution, drift, correlations and other inputs.

uA = s / √n
ucal = a / √3
uc = √(uA² + ucal²)

Type A and Type B describe evaluation methods. They do not mean “random” and “systematic,” and do not by themselves say what averaging will reduce.

Try it, then explain it

  1. Compare the first two presets. Which uncertainty component changes even though every reading stays the same?
  2. Change only the additive correction. What moves, and why does the scatter stay fixed?
  3. Enter 9.8, 9.9, 10.0, 10.1, 10.2. What could their acquisition order suggest that a mean alone hides?
Check your reasoning

The calibration half-width changes the shared component and the combined result. An additive correction shifts the estimate, leaving deviations from the mean unchanged. The ordered readings may suggest drift; more evidence is needed before using an independent-repeatability model.
