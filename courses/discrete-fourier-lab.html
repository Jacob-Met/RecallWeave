Predict. Change. Explain.

  1. Find the mean. Choose Constant. Predict DC, then remove it. Why does the reconstruction change from 3 to approximately 0?
  2. Keep a pair. Enter 1, 0, -1, 0. Keep pair 1. How do two magnitudes of 2 rebuild samples of amplitude 1?
  3. Find a singleton. Choose Alternating. Keep only Nyquist. Why does it need no second bin?
  4. Explain the loss. Return to the mixture. Drop a pair and use the RMS residual to describe what the reconstruction leaves out.

One convention throughout

X[k] = Σ x[n] e−i2πkn/N
y[n] = (1/N) Σselected k X[k] e+i2πkn/N

The forward sum is unscaled. The inverse divides by N. For real samples, partners k and N−k are complex conjugates, apart from numerical roundoff.

What this can tell you

It compares a finite array with a reconstruction on that same grid. Bin k represents k/N cycles per sample before interpreting negative-frequency partners. No sampling rate is supplied, so the lab does not assign hertz or infer a continuous signal.
