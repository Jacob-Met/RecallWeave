What the picture assumes

Both signals are real cosines with the same positive frequency, constant amplitudes, fixed relative phase and common scalar units. We project onto the real axis; positive phase advances a cosine in time.

ZA = A eiφ  ·  ZB = B ei(φ+δ)
R² = A² + B² + 2AB cos δ

For a nonzero resultant, the sum is one cosine of peak amplitude R. A zero resultant has no defined phase. Its cycle mean square is R²/2; this is a mathematical signal average, not a calibrated intensity. Arrow angle encodes phase, without specifying a direction in physical space.

The curves join evaluations of the stated model. These are calculated values, not measurements; this lab does not model different-frequency beats or a physical propagation medium.

Three things to try

  1. Rotate together. Keep 3 and 4 a quarter-cycle apart. Change common phase. Which numbers stay the same, and what shifts?
  2. Cancel, then disturb it. Choose the cancellation preset. Change B to 2.1. Is the remaining signal identically zero?
  3. Change the clock. Hold amplitudes and phases fixed, then double frequency. Compare the seconds axis with the unchanged phase relationships.
