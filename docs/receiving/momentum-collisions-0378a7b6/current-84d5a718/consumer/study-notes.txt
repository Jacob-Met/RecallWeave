3. Cart A (2 kg, +3 m/s) approaches cart B (1 kg, 0 m/s) from the left. An ideal elastic collision conserves momentum and translational kinetic energy, and the carts separate afterward. Which pair of final velocities (vA, vB) is consistent with this event?

First try: correct

5. A 1 kg cart moves at +4 m/s and a 2 kg cart moves at -2 m/s. What are their total signed momentum p and total translational kinetic energy K?

Explanation: The momenta are +4 and -4 kg m/s, so their sum is zero. Their kinetic energies are (1/2) × 1 × 4² = 8 J and (1/2) × 2 × (-2)² = 4 J. Kinetic energy is nonnegative for each positive mass, so the total is 12 J even though the total momentum is zero.

6. A begins to the left of B with a positive gap. Both keep their incoming velocities until contact. Which pair (uA, uB) makes the gap shrink so a future encounter is possible?

Your first answer: (-1, -3) m/s
First try: correct
Correct answer: (-1, -3) m/s
Explanation: Let the positive gap be xB - xA. Its rate of change before contact is uB - uA. For (-1, -3), this is -3 - (-1) = -2 m/s, so the gap shrinks. B moves left faster than A and can catch it. Motion in the same direction does not by itself rule out a collision.

8. Two 1 kg carts approach at +3 m/s and -3 m/s. They stick and stop, reducing their total translational kinetic energy from 9 J to 0 J. Which interpretation is justified?

First try: correct
Correct answer: The 9 J has changed into other energy forms; this endpoint model does not resolve their distribution.

9. Cart A (2 kg, +3 m/s) meets cart B (1 kg, 0 m/s). They stick and move together at +2 m/s with no net external impulse. How much translational kinetic energy is converted to other forms during this idealized event?

11. Treat both carts together as one fixed-mass system. The net external impulse during their interaction is zero. Which statement must hold?
Concept: System momentum

First try: correct
Correct answer: The sum of the carts' signed momenta is unchanged.
Explanation: Internal interaction forces transfer momentum between the carts. 12. For two fixed positive masses and a fixed total momentum, why is the shared velocity of a completely inelastic endpoint the state with the least translational kinetic energy?
Concept: Energy accounting
Your first answer: Any relative motion adds nonnegative kinetic energy above the center-of-mass motion, and this extra term is zero only at a shared velocity.
First try: correct
Correct answer: Any relative motion adds nonnegative kinetic energy above the center-of-mass motion, and this extra term is zero only at a shared velocity.
Explanation: Write M = mA + mB, P for the fixed total momentum, and μ = mA mB / M. The total translational kinetic energy is P²/(2M) + (μ/2)(vA - vB)². The first term is fixed. Because both masses are positive, the second is nonnegative and vanishes exactly when vA = vB = P/M. A shared velocity need not be zero.
Apply the idea: Use the energy decomposition to explain why nonzero total momentum prevents the two-cart system from having zero total translational kinetic energy.
