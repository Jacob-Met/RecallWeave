What the curves mean

These are fictional initial-rate calculations for a simple, reversible inhibition model: fixed enzyme amount, fixed temperature and pH, negligible substrate depletion and product accumulation during the initial-rate measurement, and inhibition already equilibrated. The curves do not describe reaction progress over time.

Pure noncompetitive is the equal-inhibition-constant special case. It is an extension for comparison, not a kinetic label inferred from the course’s allosteric-binding example. Binding at a separate site alone does not identify a rate law.

No cooperativity, substrate inhibition, irreversible or slow-binding inhibition, pathway feedback dynamics, or equilibrium composition is calculated.

Equations and parameter meanings

Let S be substrate concentration and r = I/Ki. With Vmax = 10 and Km = 1:

Uninhibited: v = 10 S / (1 + S)

Competitive: v = 10 S / (1 + r + S)

Pure noncompetitive: v = (10 / (1 + r)) S / (1 + S)

Km is the concentration giving half the limiting rate in a Michaelis–Menten curve; it is not generally a binding-equilibrium constant. The listed values use the fictional units shown above.
