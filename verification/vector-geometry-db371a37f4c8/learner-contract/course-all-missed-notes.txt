1. A marker first moves by u = (3, -1), then by v = (-2, 4), using the same fixed coordinate axes. What single displacement has the same final effect?
Concept: Displacement
Your first answer: (5, -5)
First try: needs review
Correct answer: (1, 3)
Explanation: Add matching components: u + v = (3 + (-2), -1 + 4) = (1, 3). The second move partly cancels the first move's x component and more than cancels its negative y component. Subtraction would compare the moves instead of combining them. The vector describes the net change in position, not the total distance traveled along both legs.
Apply the idea: Start at (4, 2) and apply the two moves. Then apply the single net move to the same starting point. Check that the final positions agree, while the lengths of the traveled paths need not agree.
Practice answer: (1, 3)
Practice result: correct on retry

2. A displacement is v = (4, -3). The unit vector d = (0, 1) points along positive y. The signed component of v along d is v·d. What is that signed component?
Concept: Dot products
Your first answer: 3
First try: needs review
Correct answer: -3
Explanation: Compute 4(0) + (-3)(1) = -3. The negative sign says the displacement goes opposite to d in the y direction. A signed component is not the full length: v has length 5, and its x component is 4. The direction vector must have length 1 for this dot product to equal the signed component; using a longer direction vector would scale the dot product.
Apply the idea: Use d = (0, -1) instead. Explain why the same physical displacement now has signed component +3 along the new direction, without changing its length.
Practice answer: 3
Practice result: keep reviewing

3. Let u = (2, 1) and v = (-1, 2). Their Euclidean dot product is u·v = u_x v_x + u_y v_y. What is u·v?
Concept: Dot products
Your first answer: 4
First try: needs review
Correct answer: 0
Explanation: Multiply matching components and add: 2(-1) + 1(2) = -2 + 2 = 0. Because both vectors are nonzero, a zero Euclidean dot product means they are perpendicular. The negative contribution from one axis cancels the positive contribution from the other; taking absolute values first would lose that information.
Apply the idea: Replace v with (1, 2). Compute the new dot product and decide whether the two nonzero vectors still meet at a right angle.
Practice answer: 0
Practice result: correct on retry

4. A marker moves from P = (1, -2) to Q = (5, 1) in a Cartesian plane. The first coordinate is x and the second is y. What is the displacement Q - P?
Concept: Displacement
Your first answer: (-4, -3)
First try: needs review
Correct answer: (4, 3)
Explanation: Subtract the starting coordinate from the ending coordinate in each component: Q - P = (5 - 1, 1 - (-2)) = (4, 3). The marker moves 4 units in the positive x direction and 3 in the positive y direction. Adding the positions gives (6, -1), which is not this displacement. Reversing the subtraction gives the move from Q back to P.
Apply the idea: Move the whole diagram by (10, 7): P becomes (11, 5) and Q becomes (15, 8). Find the displacement again. Explain why translating both endpoints leaves the displacement unchanged.
Practice answer: (-4, -3)
Practice result: keep reviewing

5. A linear map T sends e1 = (1, 0) to (2, 1), and e2 = (0, 1) to (-1, 3). Since (3, 2) = 3e1 + 2e2, what is T(3, 2)?
Concept: Matrix actions
Your first answer: (3, 2)
First try: needs review
Correct answer: (4, 9)
Explanation: Linearity preserves the weighted sum: T(3e1 + 2e2) = 3T(e1) + 2T(e2). Thus the output is 3(2, 1) + 2(-1, 3) = (6, 3) + (-2, 6) = (4, 9). Simply adding the two images forgets the coefficients 3 and 2. With column-vector multiplication, the images of e1 and e2 are the matrix's columns, not its rows.
Apply the idea: Use the same two basis-vector images to find T(-1, 2). Explain why a linear map's action on a basis determines its action on every vector in that plane.
Practice answer: (4, 9)
Practice result: correct on retry

6. A matrix M has first row (2, 1) and second row (0, 3). It multiplies the column vector v = (1, -2); each output component is the dot product of one row with v. What is Mv?
Concept: Matrix actions
Your first answer: (4, -6)
First try: needs review
Correct answer: (0, -6)
Explanation: The first output is 2(1) + 1(-2) = 0. The second is 0(1) + 3(-2) = -6. Therefore Mv = (0, -6). A matrix acts on both components together; it is not a vector to add to v. Stating that v is a column vector fixes which row products are intended and avoids silently switching conventions.
Apply the idea: Apply the same matrix to (1, 0) and to (0, 1). Relate the two outputs to the columns of M, then use them to reconstruct the output for (1, -2).
Practice answer: (4, -6)
Practice result: keep reviewing

7. A straight line through the origin has direction a = (2, 1). For b = (3, 4), which point is the orthogonal projection of b onto that line, using Euclidean distance?
Concept: Projection
Your first answer: (3, 4)
First try: needs review
Correct answer: (4, 2)
Explanation: For a nonzero direction a, the projection is p = ((b·a)/(a·a))a. Here b·a = 3(2) + 4(1) = 10 and a·a = 2^2 + 1^2 = 5, so p = 2a = (4, 2). This lies on the line. The residual b - p = (-1, 2) has dot product -2 + 2 = 0 with a, confirming that the remaining displacement is perpendicular. Dividing only by a component, or treating a as a unit vector, changes the answer.
Apply the idea: Replace the line direction a by (4, 2), which describes the same line. Recompute the formula and explain why the projected point remains (4, 2).
Practice answer: (4, 2)
Practice result: correct on retry

8. The orthogonal projection of b = (5, -1) onto the line y = x is p = (2, 2). Which residual b - p is perpendicular to that line?
Concept: Projection
Your first answer: (-3, 3)
First try: needs review
Correct answer: (3, -3)
Explanation: Subtract the projected point from the original vector: b - p = (5 - 2, -1 - 2) = (3, -3). Its dot product with line direction (1, 1) is 3 - 3 = 0. Also p + (b - p) reconstructs b. The vector (-3, 3) is perpendicular too, but it is p - b, not the requested residual. Perpendicularity alone does not determine the sign of the residual.
Apply the idea: Check both conditions for a proposed projection: the proposed point lies on the line, and the original vector minus that point is perpendicular to the line. Why is checking only one condition insufficient?
Practice answer: (-3, 3)
Practice result: keep reviewing

9. In a plane with perpendicular x and y axes measured in the same units, a displacement is v = (-3, 4). What is its Euclidean length, in those units?
Concept: Length and direction
Your first answer: 7
First try: needs review
Correct answer: 5
Explanation: The straight-line length is sqrt((-3)^2 + 4^2) = sqrt(9 + 16) = 5. The sign of an individual component changes direction, not the square used for length. The value 7 is the sum of the absolute components, and 25 is the squared Euclidean length; neither is the Euclidean length asked for.
Apply the idea: Compare the length of (-3, 4) with (3, 4) and (6, -8). Which change only reverses a component's direction, and which scales the entire vector's length?
Practice answer: 5
Practice result: correct on retry

10. A flattening map F sends every point (x, y) to (x, 0). Which pair of distinct inputs produces the same output, showing that the original input cannot always be recovered uniquely?
Concept: Order and information
Your first answer: (3, 1) and (5, 1)
First try: needs review
Correct answer: (3, 1) and (3, 5)
Explanation: F(3, 1) = (3, 0) and F(3, 5) = (3, 0). The map keeps x but discards y. In fact every point (3, y) gives that same output, so no inverse function on the whole plane can recover the original point from the output alone. The other proposed pairs have different x values and therefore produce different outputs. This flattening is a projection onto the x axis; being linear does not make a map invertible.
Apply the idea: Compare F(x, y) = (x, 0) with G(x, y) = (2x, 3y). Write a rule that reverses G and identify the division that prevents the same kind of rule from reversing F on the whole plane.
Practice answer: (3, 1) and (5, 1)
Practice result: keep reviewing

11. Use +x to the right and +y upward. A quarter-turn counterclockwise about the origin sends (x, y) to (-y, x). A horizontal stretch sends (x, y) to (2x, y). Starting at (1, 2), first apply the quarter-turn, then the stretch. Where does the point end?
Concept: Order and information
Your first answer: (4, -1)
First try: needs review
Correct answer: (-4, 1)
Explanation: The quarter-turn sends (1, 2) to (-2, 1). Applying the stretch to that result gives (-4, 1). Reversing the operations gives a different point: stretch first to (2, 2), then rotate to (-2, 2). With column vectors, the requested composition is S(Rv) = (SR)v: the rightmost operation acts first. Individual linear operations can each be well-defined while their order still matters.
Apply the idea: Try the two orders from (2, 1). Then consider a uniform stretch (x, y) → (2x, 2y). Explain why this different stretch commutes with the quarter-turn, although the horizontal-only stretch does not.
Practice answer: (-4, 1)
Practice result: correct on retry

12. A nonzero vector v = (-3, 4) has Euclidean length 5. Which vector has length 1 and points in the same direction as v?
Concept: Length and direction
Your first answer: (-3, 4)
First try: needs review
Correct answer: (-3/5, 4/5)
Explanation: Divide every component by the positive length: v / 5 = (-3/5, 4/5). Its squared length is 9/25 + 16/25 = 1, and dividing by a positive number preserves direction. The opposite signs point backward. Dividing by 7 uses the wrong length; leaving the components unchanged keeps length 5. A zero vector has no direction to preserve and cannot be normalized this way.
Apply the idea: Normalize (6, 8), then multiply the unit vector by 12. What length does the resulting vector have, and why should its direction be unchanged?
Practice answer: (-3, 4)
Practice result: keep reviewing
