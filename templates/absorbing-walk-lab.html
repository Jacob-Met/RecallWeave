When does the walk stop?

Follow exact probability mass to two absorbing boundaries. Separate the arrivals on one step from everything that has arrived so far.

A small walk, three different questions

At an interior state, move right with probability p or left with 1−p. At either endpoint, stay there. T counts steps until the first boundary visit; starting there gives T=0.

Exact distributions, not sampled paths.
This hypothetical model keeps every probability as a reduced fraction. A finite horizon limits the trace, not the eventual stopping-time calculation.

Arrival is not accumulation

First arrival counts only new boundary hits on that exact step. Cumulative absorption also includes all earlier hits. The highlighted row is the selected step.

How long, on average?

The finite observation sums survival before the selected step. Expected excess is averaged over all paths; it is not the remaining time conditioned on still being inside.
